# Six-customer route-planning case study

## Working title and research question

**A GIS-based delivery route-planning prototype for Mwihoko and surrounding
areas: shortest-distance and time-aware sequencing for six customers.**

How do the distance-minimizing and time-aware delivery orders compare for
one warehouse and six delivery locations on an OpenStreetMap driving graph,
when estimated leg times depend on vehicle, hour, and weekday?

## Network and method

The locations W, A, B, C, D, E, and F are retained in
`data/raw/delivery_locations_6_customers.csv`; the original four-location
source file is not overwritten. A fresh buffered OSMnx `drive` graph was
downloaded on 2026-11-03 and stored separately. Nearest road nodes and
haversine snap distances are in `snapped_delivery_locations_6_customers.csv`.
Snaps over 100 m are flagged. Naivas Githurai 44 (D), at the retained
coordinate `(-1.1987526752, 36.9005119783)`, is 110.2 m from nearest node
`13954735099` (`-1.1988339, 36.8995239`). The node connects to a
`highway=residential` edge without explicit access/service tags. A closer
mapped road line is Kamiti Road (`highway=secondary`) at about 78.0 m from
the supplied point. The nearest node and nearest road segment are different
snap targets, accounting for the apparent discrepancy. The graph does not
contain a verified Naivas vehicle entrance or store footprint at the supplied
point, so the original coordinate remains unchanged and the >100 m warning
remains. The road class suggests a general motor-vehicle road, but access to
the specific delivery entrance needs field verification. A diagnostic map
and report are generated by `python src/diagnose_naivas_snap.py` into
`visualizations/naivas_snap_diagnostic.html` and
`visualizations/naivas_snap_diagnostic.csv`. All 42 directed pairs were
reachable in the extracted graph.

NetworkX Dijkstra uses edge length to find each directed leg's shortest
distance. The optimizer exhaustively evaluates 720 permutations of the six
customers, starts and ends each tour at W, sums its seven legs, and ranks the
results. The current shortest tour is W → F → E → D → C → B → A → W at
21.909 km. The top five are saved in
`data/processed/route_optimization_results_6_customers.csv`.

The time optimizer evaluates the same 720 orders. It predicts each leg with
the trained ETA model and advances the weekday/hour by the predicted
travel duration before evaluating the next leg. The selected objective
determines the tour ranking; each displayed road leg remains the
shortest-distance path between its endpoints.

## Simulated ETA model

The generator creates 42 × 7 × 24 × 5 × 3 = 105,840 rows with eight model
features and the simulated duration target. A separate `replicate_id`
labels the five seeded noisy repetitions, and is excluded from features.
Assumptions: motorcycle 30 km/h, car 25 km/h, van 22 km/h; hourly and
weekday multipliers; 0.04 minutes of delay per edge; Gaussian noise with
seed 42. These do not represent measured local traffic.

Models retained: median baseline, Linear Regression, Random Forest, and
Gradient Boosting. Model comparisons include the repeated-scenario grouped
train/test split, directional-route holdout, and four-fold GroupKFold by
directed route. MAE, RMSE, and R² are reported. Final selection uses the
mean rank across holdout MAE and mean fold CV MAE; R² is not used for
selection. Tree model sizes were reduced for the expanded dataset: 20-tree
Random Forest (depth 14, leaf size 3) and 25-tree depth-2 Gradient Boosting.

Linear Regression was selected. Its unseen-route holdout MAE was 1.280 min
(RMSE 1.780, R² 0.955); route-level cross-validation mean-fold MAE was
1.221 min (pooled RMSE 1.626, R² 0.961). Random Forest was close at 1.410
min holdout MAE and 1.240 min CV mean-fold MAE. On the repeated-scenario
split, Random Forest had slightly lower MAE (1.175 vs. 1.195 min), but
Linear Regression had lower RMSE and won both route-generalization checks.
These scores describe fit
to synthetic labels only; real-world ETA accuracy has not been established.

## Limitations

Only six fixed customers and a single warehouse are represented. OSM
coverage and coordinate snapping affect results. ETA inputs include
hour-of-day but are simulated. The time-based tour search does not optimize
alternate road paths by travel time. Service time, windows, capacity,
closures, and stochastic arrival uncertainty are not modeled. The Naivas
coordinate/snap needs manual confirmation before operational use.
